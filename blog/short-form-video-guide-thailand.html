Short-Form Video in Thailand: A Practical Guide to TikTok, Reels and Shorts for Brands

Short-form video is now the fastest way for a brand to be discovered. The good news: it rewards ideas and consistency more than big budgets.

Vertical, short videos have become the default way people discover products, restaurants and services. For brands in Thailand, that is an opportunity: a well-planned 20-second video can reach people who have never heard of you. Here is a practical approach that works whether you film on a phone or with a full production team.

The first two seconds decide everything

Viewers decide almost instantly whether to keep watching. Open with movement, a surprising visual or a clear promise. Avoid slow logo intros.

  • Start with the result: the finished dish, the fitted suit, the product in action.
  • Ask a question your customer is already thinking.
  • Use on-screen text so the video works with the sound off.

Formats that work for businesses

Behind the scenes

Show how things are made: the kitchen during service, the tailor's fitting process, the team preparing a shoot. People trust what they can see.

Before and after

Transformations are naturally satisfying to watch, from interiors and haircuts to product setups.

Product in real life

Show the product being used in a real situation rather than on a white background. For a smartwatch, that might be a morning run; for a sauce, a family dinner.

Answer a common question

Turn the questions your staff hear every day into short, helpful videos. They also make great content for your website and LINE OA.

Plan in batches, not one by one

The brands that post consistently plan and film several videos in one session. A simple monthly workflow:

  1. List ten ideas based on customer questions, products and upcoming events.
  2. Write a one-line hook and a short shot list for each.
  3. Film everything in one or two sessions, with good light and clean sound.
  4. Edit in batches, add captions and schedule posts across the month.
✦

Tip. Film more than you need: extra clips become cut-downs, stories and ads later.

Phone or professional production?

Both have a place. Phone-filmed, authentic videos are ideal for regular social posting. Professionally produced video, with planned shots, lighting, talent and colour grading, is worth it for product launches, brand films and paid campaigns where quality directly affects perception. Many brands combine the two: one professional shoot that produces a hero video plus dozens of short cut-downs.

Make it work with the sound off

Many people scroll on public transport, at work or late at night with the sound off. Burned-in captions, clear on-screen text and visual storytelling make sure your message lands either way. In Thailand, consider bilingual captions if you are reaching both Thai and international viewers.

Simple production basics that lift quality

  • Light: face a window or use a soft light; avoid strong light from behind.
  • Sound: a small clip-on microphone is the cheapest big upgrade for talking videos.
  • Stability: use a tripod or gimbal unless the shaky handheld look is intentional.
  • Framing: shoot vertically (9:16) and keep key details away from the edges where app buttons sit.
  • Pace: cut out pauses; a new shot every one to three seconds keeps attention.

Trends: use them, don't chase them

Trending sounds and formats can boost reach, but they move fast and do not always fit a brand. Use a trend when it naturally suits your product and voice, and adapt it rather than copying it. Your evergreen content, such as how-tos, behind the scenes and product demos, will keep working long after a trend has faded.

The best-performing brand videos rarely look like ads. They look like something a viewer would have chosen to watch anyway.

Measure what matters

Views are only the beginning. Look at how long people watch, how many share or save, and, most importantly, whether the video drives profile visits, messages, bookings or sales.

  • Watch time and completion: tells you whether the hook and pacing work.
  • Shares and saves: show the content is genuinely valuable.
  • Profile visits, link clicks and messages: connect content to business results.

Review your top and bottom five videos each month and look for patterns in hooks, topics and length. Then make more of what works. If you need help with a launch or campaign, see our video production service, or read how to turn video into sales with live selling.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a short-form video be?

Most perform well between 15 and 45 seconds. Make it as short as the idea allows, and keep the pace up.

Can I use the same video on TikTok, Reels and Shorts?

Yes, with small adjustments. Remove watermarks, check that text is not covered by each app's interface, and adapt captions to each audience.

Do I need professional video production?

Not for everything. Use professional production for launches, ads and brand films, and phone content for regular posting.

How often should a brand post short videos?

Consistency matters more than volume. Two to four good videos a week is a realistic target for many businesses, especially when they are planned and filmed in batches.
